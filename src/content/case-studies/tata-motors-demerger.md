## The situation

Tata Motors combined three very different businesses: an Indian commercial vehicle franchise that generates cash and follows the economic cycle, a domestic passenger vehicle and EV business that consumes capital, and Jaguar Land Rover. Investors who wanted exposure to only one of these had to own all three, and a single valuation multiple fitted none of them well.

## How the deal was structured

- **Vertical split along business lines.** The CV business was demerged into a separately listed company. The passenger vehicle, EV and JLR businesses stayed together in Tata Motors Passenger Vehicles.
- **1:1 entitlement.** Shareholders on the 14 October 2025 record date received one CV share for each share they held, so ownership was unchanged on day one.
- **Special pre-listing session.** The exchanges' price-discovery session set reference prices of ₹400 for PV and ₹261 for CV, giving the market its first separate values for the two businesses.

## Valuation logic

A sum-of-the-parts valuation lets each business trade against its natural peers. CV can be compared with Indian truck and bus makers on a cyclical EV/EBITDA multiple. PV and EV can be compared with domestic passenger vehicle makers, and JLR with global premium OEMs. If the parts are worth more separately than the combined company traded for, the split unlocks value. It also makes it easier to raise capital or bring in a partner for one business without diluting the other.

## Lessons for bankers

1. **Demergers are an alternative to selling a business.** When a division is mis-valued inside a group, separating it can unlock value without a buyer or any tax leakage.
2. **Prepare the equity story for each entity before listing.** Each business needs its own management team, capital allocation policy and investor-relations plan ready in advance.
3. **Expect index and passive-fund flows to move the prices.** In the early weeks, index inclusion and passive rebalancing can drive trading in the new entities as much as fundamentals do.
4. **Use it as a template.** Other Indian conglomerates with businesses on different cycles, such as capital goods alongside consumer, or defence alongside commercial, can follow the same approach.
